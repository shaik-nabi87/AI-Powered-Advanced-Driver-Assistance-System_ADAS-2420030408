## Abstract

Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) have transformed modern vehicle safety by enabling real-time perception, collision warning, lane monitoring, autonomous emergency braking, and other active safety functions. However, most existing ADAS technologies are designed primarily for the **pre-crash phase** and provide limited intelligence after a serious collision occurs. Once an accident takes place, critical video, vehicle telemetry, and sensor information may be lost, while emergency response and accident investigation often depend on manual reporting or limited telematics data. This limitation creates a significant gap between **collision prevention and post-crash response**.

This project presents **SAAHAS-ADAS (System for Automated Accident Harvesting, Analysis, and Support in ADAS)**, an integrated AI-based framework that extends ADAS capabilities from collision prevention to **post-crash incident intelligence, digital evidence preservation, forensic reconstruction, automated documentation, and emergency response**. The system continuously collects synchronized information from vehicle-mounted cameras, Inertial Measurement Units (IMU), Controller Area Network (CAN) telemetry, airbag status, and Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) sensors. A multi-signal crash confirmation mechanism evaluates airbag deployment, vehicle deceleration, high-G acceleration, severe-impact signals, and visual anomalies to reliably identify a collision while reducing false detections.

When a crash is confirmed, SAAHAS-ADAS automatically preserves a synchronized pre- and post-crash evidence window containing video streams, vehicle kinematics, braking and steering information, and geographic and temporal data. The captured evidence is secured through **SHA-256 hashing and hardware-backed digital signatures**, providing tamper-evident protection and supporting the digital chain of custody. The preserved data is then analyzed by an **AI Incident Intelligence Engine**, which combines computer-vision analysis with vehicle telemetry to identify important accident characteristics such as vehicle deformation, rollover conditions, pedestrian involvement, barrier collisions, and fire or smoke indicators. Kinematic information is further used for crash-severity estimation, while the complete sensor history is analyzed to reconstruct a chronological accident timeline from the initial hazard and driver response through ADAS intervention, collision, and the final resting state of the vehicle.

Based on this reconstructed incident intelligence, the system generates two primary outputs. The first is an **AI-assisted First Information Report (FIR) and accident dossier**, containing structured information about the accident time, location, involved parties, road conditions, mechanical-failure checks, visual observations, and confidence information for subsequent verification by authorized investigators. The second is a **context-rich emergency dispatch packet** containing critical information such as vehicle occupancy, estimated crash severity, rollover status, trapped-occupant probability, and precise incident location, enabling emergency responders to receive more useful information than conventional location-only crash notifications.

SAAHAS-ADAS also incorporates an **offline-resilient architecture** to maintain essential functionality in areas with unreliable or unavailable network connectivity. In online conditions, edge and cloud resources can support incident analysis and rapid transmission of emergency information, while during network outages, local edge processing and secure in-vehicle storage preserve evidence and generate baseline incident information. The stored data can subsequently be synchronized when network connectivity is restored. Privacy protection, role-based access control, and immutable audit logging are incorporated to protect sensitive video, vehicle, occupant, and location information.

Overall, SAAHAS-ADAS establishes a unified **prevention–detection–forensics–response ecosystem** that enables a vehicle to remain an intelligent participant beyond the moment of impact. By combining multi-sensor crash detection, secure evidence harvesting, AI-driven incident reconstruction, automated forensic documentation, and context-aware emergency communication, the system provides a comprehensive approach to improving **post-crash situational awareness, evidence integrity, emergency response, and accident investigation**.
